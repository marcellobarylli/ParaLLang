*The creation myth for "language is the fabric of reality". Tone: [[GAME MECHANICS#Terry Pratchett × meme culture, absurd humour|Pratchett × meme culture]]. Written 2026-11-19 by Claude at the owner's request, as a first draft to edit.*

# The Telling

## The myth

In the beginning was the Word.

This is generally agreed. What is not agreed is which word it was, and four thousand years of scholarship have narrowed it down to two schools, which do not speak to each other. This is traditional.

The first school holds that the Word was **"Once"**. The second holds that it was **"Well?"** Both agree on the important point, which is that it was not the kind of word that can be left alone. You cannot say "Once" and stop. The silence afterwards has an expectant quality. It taps its foot.

The difficulty, as every first-year student points out and every professor has learned to talk over, is that a word needs somebody to say it and somebody to hear it, and at the time there was nobody at all. The accepted answer is that the Word said itself. This is the sort of thing that is only allowed to happen once, and the universe has been filing paperwork about it ever since.\*

So the Word was said, and it hung there in the nothing, and the nothing waited. And because no word can bear to be the only one in the room, it said another. Then another. It was not creating. It was *filling an awkward pause*, and it has not managed to stop.

Things came to be because they were mentioned. There was light because the story had reached a point where somebody would need to read by it. There was ground because the Telling said "stood", and you cannot stand without it. There was time because of the word "then". The sea is the place where the Telling said "and so on". Mountains are emphasis.

Nothing was made that was not first said. This is why a thing with no name is so hard to find, and why a thing with two names is so dangerous.

For a long while (there was now such a thing as a while) there was one Telling, and everything in it understood everything else, and it was extremely dull.

Then somebody **retold it**.

Nobody knows who. The retelling was nearly perfect. One word came out a little differently: a vowel leaned, a meaning slipped half a step to the left. And where the two versions disagreed, the world could not be both, so it became two. That was the first branch. Every branch since has grown the same way, from a story told again by somebody who was sure they remembered how it went.

So the worlds are not siblings. They are *versions*. Each language is a retelling of the Telling, and each believes, with total sincerity, that it is the original and the others are doing an accent.

And then somebody **tried to help**.

They stood between two versions and said, kindly, "What she *means* is…" That was the first translation, and it left the first stitch: a thread from one branch to the other, with a very slight pull. Tradition blames Journathan. Journathan blames tradition.†

As for who said the Word, if the Word did not say itself: the old story is that a philosopher once put this to a market woman, to show her the flaw in her beliefs. *If the world is a story,* he said, *someone must be telling it. And who tells the teller?* She looked at him with the patience of a person who sells fish to the public. "Somebody in a bigger story," she said. *And who tells that one?* "You're very clever, young man," she said, "but it's stories all the way up."

She was right, and she had not finished. It is stories all the way *down* as well. Everything that is told can tell. A book written inside the Telling is a small world with its own ground and its own light, and the people in it are quite certain they are real, and are writing books.

It is said that the Telling will end the way all tellings end: not with a full stop, which is only a pause that has given up, but with a **correction**. One day somebody will helpfully point out a mistake in the first Word, and they will be right, and that will be that. The slip of paper has already been printed. It is headed *Errata*.

Until then the only people who can see the page are the ones who cannot yet read it. To everyone born in a story, the words are just the world. It takes a foreigner to notice the print.

*\* The Bureau of First Causes has ruled that a word may not be its own author, and has written to the Word about this several times. The letters come back marked "addressee is the alphabet".*

*† He maintains he was only asking for directions.*

## What the myth fixes in the lore

- **The origin of branching.** [[LORE]] asks what generates a branch. Here: a branch is a *retelling*. A language splits where the story is told again and a word comes out differently. That mirrors how real languages drift, without needing people to migrate across land
- **The first stitch.** The counterforce is the first *translation*. It keeps [[CHARACTERS#Journathan|Journathan]] as the suspect without settling it
- **Why naming is power.** "Nothing was made that was not first said." Learning a word is getting hold of the thing. A thing with two names (a translated thing) is where the tension, and the spell, comes from
- **The fish and the water.** The last paragraph is the [[LORE#4th wall|4th wall]] conceit as scripture: natives cannot see the print, the learner can
- **Errata.** The boss of the Rift already has the name. The myth makes her the prophesied end: the correction slip for the first Word. Collision is the same event at the scale of two branches: the text stops meaning anything

## Stories all the way down (the nesting)

The owner's idea: everything is a story within a story within a story, like the Painted World of Ariamis in Dark Souls, where a painting holds a world. Painted form there, written form here.

- **Up:** the Telling may itself be a book in a bigger story, in a language nobody here has heard. The oldest [[LORE#Infohazards|infohazards]] and the "eldritch root language" could be fragments of the *narrating* language leaking down: words from the level above, which this level is not built to hold
- **Down:** a book written inside this world is a smaller world. *The Journey* is one. That is why nobody remembers whether Journathan or his book came first: from inside, the question has no answer. The [[LORE#4th wall|spores]] are the same mechanism in nature: dream a new tongue, wake in the world that speaks it
- **Sideways:** the branches. Versions of the same level
- So there are three ways to travel: across (translate), down (read or write yourself into a text), up (get read). The player already does the third, from outside
- **Level design hook.** The prototype stage is already "Painted World Style" ([[LOCATIONS]]). The Crumbling Market can be a *page*: a small written world inside the main one, entered through a book, a sign or an inscription. Cheap to build and reuses assets, which fits New Game Plus on reused places: each pass is a rereading, and a reread text means more
- **Mechanic hook.** Texts found inside a level could be enterable later as their own short levels in harder language. Difficulty by depth: each level down is told in less of the player's own language

## Where to tell it in the game

- Never whole. Fragments of *The Journey*, inscriptions and containment files each carry a few lines, in the target language, and contradict each other on the Word ("Once" against "Well?")
- The two schools are a running joke and a faction split. Portia would hold a firm view and a form to register it on
- The market woman's line is the one to keep in English and in every pack: "It's stories all the way up"

## Parallels (the sources the owner named, checked)

- **"In the beginning was the Word"** is the opening of the Gospel of John (1:1). The Bible itself opens with Genesis 1:1, "In the beginning God created…", where God then creates by speaking ("Let there be light", 1:3). John's opening deliberately echoes Genesis. Both serve the idea: creation by speech
- **Tolkien, the Ainulindalë** (the first part of *The Silmarillion*): the Ainur sing a great Music on themes given by Eru Ilúvatar, Melkor's discord is woven in, Ilúvatar shows them the Music as a vision, and the world becomes real when he says one word, "Eä". So even there it ends on a word. Melkor's discord is a model for the first retelling: the error is part of the composition
- **Dark Souls, the Painted World of Ariamis:** a world inside a painting, a refuge for what has no place outside it. Painted form to our written form
- **"Turtles all the way down":** only the shape of the saying is used (the market woman). No turtles, no elephants
