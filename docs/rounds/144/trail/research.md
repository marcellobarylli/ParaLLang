## 1. Scope and sequence, A1 to B1

**CEFR global scale [1]**
- A1 [S]: "Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases aimed at the satisfaction of
  needs of a concrete type."
- A2 [S]: "frequently used expressions related to areas of most immediate relevance (e.g. very basic personal and
  family information, shopping, local geography, employment)."
- B1 [S]: "Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters regularly encountered in work,
  school, leisure, etc."

**Spoken interaction, self-assessment grid [2]**
- A1 [S]: "I can interact in a simple way provided the other person is prepared to repeat or rephrase things at a
  slower rate of speech".
- A2 [S]: "I can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring a simple and direct exchange of information on
  familiar topics and activities."
- B1 [S]: "I can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling in an area where the language is spoken."
- Companion Volume conversation-scale wording: [U].

**Order of functions (synthesis)**
- Supporting extracts [S]: Zaragoza A1 lists "Dar y pedir datos personales", "Saludar y despedirse", "Expresar
  intenciones" [6]; an Istituto Italiano di Cultura A1 outline lists "Salutare e presentarsi" [10]; Duolingo says its
  CEFR courses start with phrases like "Coffee, please!" [13].
- Usual order, from those plus the CEFR levels:
  - A1: greet and introduce; personal data, numbers, spelling; ordering and buying; likes; family and descriptions;
    location and directions; time and daily routine; plans.
  - A2: past events and narration; comparing; arrangements and invitations; simple future; health, travel and
    service transactions.
  - B1: opinions with reasons; advice, wishes, hypotheses; unprepared conversation; problems while travelling.
- No single syllabus gave this full order: [U] as a complete sequence.

**Spanish grammar order (named syllabi)**
- Complutense (UCM) A1.1 [3] [S]: noun gender, definite and indefinite articles, demonstratives, present indicative
  including irregular *ser, estar, haber, ir*; uses of *ser* vs *estar*; *estar/hay*; *gustar*.
- UCM A2 [4] [S]: "Pretérito indefinido. Forma: Paradigma de las formas regulares de las tres conjugaciones." B1
  returns to it: "Irregularidades vocálicas y consonánticas".
- Málaga (UMA) [5] [S]: early levels practise "pretérito imperfecto, imperativo" and revise "pretérito perfecto,
  pretérito indefinido y futuro simple"; then "pluscuamperfecto, condicional, presente de subjuntivo". B1 revises the
  present subjunctive and adds imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive. The exact level boundary for the present
  subjunctive (A2+ or B1) was unclear in the extract.
- Zaragoza [6] [S]: indefinido vs imperfecto contrast at A2.
- UCM's B2 programme is where the extracts show subjunctive tenses listed [4] [S]; its B1 verb section was not visible.
- Instituto Cervantes Plan Curricular inventories: [U], not retrieved.

**Italian grammar order (named syllabi)**
- Bolzano A1 [7] [S]: present indicative, *essere/avere*, modals, and passato prossimo already at A1, with auxiliary
  choice but "non è richiesto l'accordo del participio passato con il soggetto".
- CILS (Siena) contents [8] [S]: A1 includes passato prossimo, infinitive, imperative. A2 adds productive passato
  prossimo plus imperfetto. B1 lists "indicativo passato prossimo; indicativo imperfetto; condizionale presente". The
  congiuntivo (presente e imperfetto) and condizionale passato appear at B2.
- Other A2 syllabi [9] [S]: "opposizione imperfetto e passato prossimo" (Bergamo); futuro semplice (Firenze, Padova).
- CELI 2 (B1, Perugia) [10] [S]: narrating in present, past and future; conditional for polite requests; combined
  pronouns; *ci/ne*.
- Padova B1 [9] [S]: condizionale presente, congiuntivo presente, combined and relative pronouns.
- Profilo della lingua italiana and the PLIDA syllabus: [U], not retrieved.

**Cross-language note (synthesis):** Italian syllabi put a perfect past tense at A1; the Spanish syllabi seen put
the indefinido at A2. The subjunctive is B1 in university syllabi for both languages, but productive only at B2 in
CILS.

## 2. How Duolingo and others scale a course

**Structure**
- The path launched 1 November 2022 [11] [S]. "One level (each circle in the path) is equivalent to one crown level
  of a skill" [11] [S].
- Levels mix new and old: "Some cover brand-new concepts and some cover previously introduced concepts you need to
  review" [11] [S].
- Review is placed on the path "by placing specific practice levels along the path" [11] [S].
- Guidebooks: [U].

**CEFR alignment and ordering**
- "Duolingo courses are aligned to the CEFR, so each section of our courses has an intended proficiency goal." [13] [S]
- They "systematically identify and define communicative objectives that vary according to language level" [13] [S].
- Vocabulary-frequency policy: [U], nothing found.
- Duolingo Score bands [14] [S]: 30–59 = A2, 60–79 = early B1, 80–99 = high B1, 100–114 = early B2, 115–129 =
  advanced B2. That 0–29 = A1 is the subagent's inference.

**Course size (unofficial tracker, not Duolingo) [15] [S]**

| Course (snapshot) | Sections | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Spanish from English (Sept 2025) | 8 | 974 total: 8 intro, 26 + 28 A1, 52 A2, 250 + 250 B1, 180 + 180 B2 |
| Italian from English (Aug 2025) | 4 | 130 total: 10 intro, 30 + 30 A1, 60 A2; 2,910 lessons |

- So roughly 114–130 units reach the end of A2, at about 22 lessons per unit for Italian (the subagent's arithmetic).
- Italian B1 sections: [U].

**Pacing and review**
- New words per lesson: no official figure found: [U].
- Half-life regression (Settles & Meeder 2016) [16] [S]: "error reduced by 45%+ compared to several baselines" and it
  could "improve Duolingo daily student engagement by 12% in an operational user study".
- Within a lesson: "at the end of a lesson, you'll review any mistakes you made" [12] [S]. Personalized practice uses
  "spaced repetition (along with accuracy)" [12] [S].
- Legendary is "a lesson with more challenging exercises and no hints" [11] [S].
- Each CEFR section ending in a quiz [13]: [S], paraphrase only.

**Birdbrain and target success rate**
- It estimates "both the difficulty of the exercise and the current proficiency of the learner" [17] [S].
- **No published target success rate was found** in three searches: [U]. The only stated rationale: "if the questions
  are too easy, people grow bored; if the questions are too hard, they become frustrated" [17] [S].

**Other products**
- Babbel [18] [S]: lessons "aligned to the ... CEFR"; "Every time you complete a lesson, new vocabulary will be added
  to your Review manager"; intervals depend on review count and accuracy. Six stages and lesson length: [U].
- Pimsleur [19] [S, secondary summaries of the 1967 paper "A Memory Schedule"]: intervals of 5 s, 25 s, 2 min, 10 min,
  1 h, 5 h, 1 day, 5 days, 25 days, 4 months, 2 years; lessons about 30 minutes. New words per lesson: [U]. One
  German-vocabulary study found "a uniform delay led to higher retention rates than a graduated delay" [19] [S].
- Michel Thomas [20] [S, publisher]: cognates, then verbs; "within the very first hour you will be able to construct
  simple phrases".
- Language Transfer [20] [S, via a review]: Complete Spanish has 90 tracks; Track 1 says "It is very important that
  you don't try to memorize anything." No peer-reviewed evidence found for either method.

## 3. Pacing evidence

**(a) Repetitions and items per session**
- Uchihara, Webb & Yanagisawa 2019 [21] [S]: "a medium effect (r = .34) of repetition on incidental vocabulary
  learning", moderated by age, vocabulary knowledge, spacing, visual support and engagement. Synthesis: there is no
  fixed "N encounters" number.
- Webb 2007 [22] [S, secondary]: 10 encounters gave large recognition gains but not enough to use the word.
- Rawson et al. 2018 [23] [S, secondary]: one correct recall in each of three spaced sessions beat three recalls in
  one session, "68% vs. 26%".
- Nation's "5–16 encounters": [U]. A recommended number of new words per session: [U], nothing authoritative found.

**(b) Frequency coverage and vocabulary size**
- Nation & Waring 1997, Brown corpus, lemmas [24] [S]: 1,000 words = 72.0%, 2,000 = 79.7%, 3,000 = 84.0%.
- Nation 2006 [25] [S]: "98% text coverage (1 unknown word in 50)" needs about 8,000–9,000 families for reading and
  6,000–7,000 for speech.
- Spanish [26] [S, secondary citing Davies 2005]: top 3,000 words cover about 94% of oral texts.
- Milton & Alexiou [27] [S]: on a 5,000-word X-Lex scale, A1 under 1,500, A2 1,500–2,500, B1 2,750–3,250. Which
  language column this is was unclear in the extract.

**(c) Optimal difficulty**
- Wilson et al. 2019 [28] [S]: "the optimal error rate for learning is 15.87%", about 85% accuracy. The scope is
  binary classification by gradient-descent learners, not human language production.
- Bjork & Linn [29] [S]: "Conditions of practice that appear optimal during instruction can fail to support long-term
  retention and transfer of knowledge".
- Roediger & Karpicke 2006 [30] [S, press release]: at one week the tested group recalled "61 percent ... as compared
  with only 40 percent".
- Cepeda 2006 [31] [S]: 839 assessments, 317 experiments; the best gap grows with the retention interval.
- Cepeda 2008 [31] [S]: optimal gap fell "from about 20 to 40% of a 1-week test delay to about 5 to 10% of a 1-year
  test delay".
- Nakata & Suzuki 2019 [32] [S]: "Although interleaving led to the highest number of incorrect responses during
  training, it was more effective than blocking in the 1‐week delayed posttest".

**(d) Nation's four strands [33] [S]**
- "roughly equal amounts of time" for each strand.
- Language-focused learning "should not make up more than one-quarter of the time".
- Fluency development is "helping the learners make the best use of what is already known".

**(e) Hours**
- Cambridge [34] [S]: A2 180–200 guided learning hours, B1 350–400, "guidelines only". A1 of 90–100: [U].
- FSI [35] [S, archived state.gov page from 2021]: Italian and Spanish are Category I, 24 weeks. The "600–750 hours"
  figure is secondary only. The FSI target is professional proficiency, well above B1.

**(f) Mastery and spacing shape**
- Slavin 1987 [36] [S]: the mastery criterion is "usually in the range of 80-90% correct"; below it, corrective
  instruction and a parallel retest (Guskey [36] [S]).
- Karpicke & Roediger 2007 [37] [S, title]: "Expanding retrieval practice promotes short-term retention, but equally
  spaced retrieval enhances long-term retention".
- Kang et al. 2014 [37] [S]: expanding practice "produced recall equivalent to that from equal-interval practice" at
  eight weeks.
- Rawson & Dunlosky 2011 "3 correct recalls + 3 relearning sessions": [U].

## 4. Design rules (all synthesis; sources in brackets)

1. **Target success rate:** tune each conversation to about 85% first-attempt success, in a band of 80–90%. Below
   75%, drop back; above 95%, add load. The 85% figure is an analogy from machine learning, and Duolingo publishes no
   number. [28][36][17]
2. **New-item budget:** A2 means about 1,500–2,500 recognised words in about 180–200 hours, so roughly 8–13 new words
   per hour of total play. Put 3–5 new productive items in a "new" conversation and none in review conversations.
   [27][34]
3. **One new grammar point per unit**, introduced as a communicative function first, in the syllabus order from
   section 1. [13][3–10]
4. **Conversation mix:** at most 25% of time on explicit new-form teaching; at least 25% on fluency conversations with
   no new items. In practice, about one new conversation to one review or fluency conversation. [33][11]
5. **"Learned" criterion per item:** correct production in at least three separate sessions, never three times in one
   sitting, and at least 10 total encounters before retiring it from active review. [23][22][21]
6. **Spacing:** re-test at about 1–3 days, then about 1 week, then about 3–5 weeks. Equal or expanding gaps are both
   acceptable; the delay matters more than the shape. [31][37][19]
7. **Interleave:** every review conversation mixes at least 2–3 earlier grammar points or units. Expect lower
   in-session accuracy and do not gate on it. [32][29]
8. **Unlock rule:** at least 80% (stretch 90%) on a unit checkpoint. On failure, give a targeted corrective
   conversation and a parallel retest. [36]
9. **Measure mastery after a delay:** score the checkpoint on a later session, not straight after teaching. [30][29]
10. **Unit length:** 6–8 conversations (3–4 new, 2–3 interleaved review or fluency, 1 checkpoint), giving about 12–20
    new items per unit. This number has weak direct support; Duolingo's Italian unit is about 22 micro-lessons. [15]
11. **Boss reviews:** a no-hint, cumulative, interleaved conversation at each unit end and a larger one at each CEFR
    boundary. [11][13]
12. **Scale and vocabulary:** B1 needs about as much content as A1 and A2 combined (350–400 vs 180–200 hours;
    Duolingo Spanish has 500 B1 units vs 114 to A2). Draw A1–A2 words from the top 1,000–2,000 lemmas. [34][15][24][26]
