## (c) Suggestions for the character at late A1

1. **One question per turn, at the end, about the player.** Drop the "what do you want to know about my X" and "what do you think of my work" tails entirely. If the character wants to invite questions, it can show something odd and wait (like "Nel cassetto ho sette zii..."), so curiosity does the work.
2. **React first, in character, to what the player actually said.** Replace generic "che bello" with the character's own take: the critic gives stars, the waiter mishears, the genealogist tries to place an uncle with your friend. The good lines above all did this.
3. **Don't recite the objective phrase. Set up a situation that needs it.** For example, the map is upside down and he turns it the wrong way (the learner has to say "a sinistra!"), or the pen has no price tag and she taps it meaningfully. Bring in at most one objective per turn, so the 3 graded replies each have work to do.
4. **Keep lines to 2-3 short sentences, simple present.** Use one funny or weather detail, not three. Avoid passato prossimo, idioms ("di bello", "per la casa", "non ci credo") and riddles. When the learner says "non capisco", rephrase with easier words, not harder ones.
5. **Keep a running thread and keep the scene consistent.** Follow up on the player's last topic (Luca's house, the river hunt, the market). Accept a proposal like "andiamo al mercato!" instead of shutting it down. Keep the weather consistent unless the change is the joke and gets commented on. Use each running gag once or twice, not every turn, and don't reuse surnames or tics across characters.
6. **Corrections:** don't take a heart for a single English word or for "ha ha" in a playful aside. Use the same new word in the correction and in the character's line (capovolta vs. sottosopra).
