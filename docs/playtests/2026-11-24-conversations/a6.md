## (c) Suggestions

- **One question per line, about the player or about what they just said.** Drop the "what do you want to know about
  my work" tail entirely; if the character wants to share their work, have them *tell* a short funny fact and let the
  player ask if they want. Never re-offer after the player has asked about the work.
- **At A1 unit 1, recycle the target words only.** After the three objectives are done, rotate through variations:
  "Ciao, Sam! Come stai?" / "Bene? Anch'io sto bene, grazie." / "Buongiorno, Brunilde! (ding)" / "Arrivederci, Sam!"
  When the learner says "non capisco", rephrase with fewer words, not a new question. When the learner says
  "arrivederci", say it back — that is the lesson.
- **A2/B1: react, then ask one follow-up on the player's thread** (Bruno, Florence, the sister, the mushrooms) and vary
  the question type: feelings (Ti è piaciuto?), comparisons, plans, weather ("Piove anche da te?"). Lazlo and Sidonie
  show the model already does this well when it isn't adding the tag.
- **Don't say goodbye at reply 3** unless the player says goodbye; just keep the conversation going so keep-chatting is
  seamless.
- **Rotate the gag**: use a running joke at most every other line, and vary the sentence frame (not "X è come Y" or
  "che bello!" every time).
- **Don't model the objective sentence before the player** (no "la lampada non funziona" up front); set the scene
  ("La camera è... interessante. Ti piace?") and let the learner find the problem.
- Keep the weather/situation narration (it worked, especially rain arriving in the Sidonie chat), but keep its Italian
  nouns within the unit's level and keep time of day consistent.
