## (a) Top 5 problems

1. **"What do you want to know / what do you think of my work" as the closing question. 22 of 35 lines (63%).** It's worst in keep-chatting: 13 of 15 keep-chatting lines end with it. The owner's complaint 1 is fully present at A2; the form has only moved from "what do you think of my X" to "what else do you want to know about my work".
   - "Che cosa vuoi sapere ancora del mio lavoro?" (time K2, K3; near-identical in K1)
   - "E tu, cosa pensi del mestiere di un poeta degli orari, e cosa vuoi sapere di me o del mio gatto?" (routine K3)
   - "Che cosa pensi del mio lavoro, e che cosa vuoi sapere ancora delle stagioni?" (weather K2)
2. **Every line ends with two (sometimes three) questions. 34 of 35 lines; 3 triples.** One good question about me usually gets paired with a self-centred one, which dilutes it. Only time R3 ended on one question, and it was the best line in that conversation.
   - "E tu, chi sei, che cosa cerchi qui di notte e che cosa pensi del mio lavoro?" (time G)
   - "E tu, che cosa hai comprato di bello, che cosa è successo la settimana scorsa e che cosa pensi del mio lavoro?" (past_did R1: reads as the objective checklist)
   - "E tu, che cosa hai fatto oggi, com'era il gatto, e che cosa vuoi sapere del mio lavoro?" (past_did K3)
3. **Verbal tics repeated every turn. About 22 lines.** "Leggo l'ultima riga del mio registro: «...»" opens 6/6 of Ambrose's replies. "Scrivo nel libro: «...»" opens 6/6 of Cuthbert's. "il mio orologio è (sempre) indietro" 4x for Gideon D. "mio cugino ha ... migliore" 5x for Griselda (this one stayed funny longest because it varied). Also across characters: two ledger-keepers, two "matita behind each ear", two Gideons, so the generator feels templated.
4. **Giving the answer away / pre-empting objectives. 6 lines.** Greetings recite the exact target phrases, and characters volunteer the info the player is supposed to ask for.
   - "Qui tutti mi chiedono: «Che tempo fa?» e «Quale stagione preferisci?»" (weather G)
   - "Io nel mio tempo libero scrivo e conto le stelle, ma tu..." (routine R2, before I'd asked objective 3)

## (c) Suggestions for this level (late A1 / A2)

1. **End with exactly one question, and make it about the player**: their day, food, pets, trip, preferences, what they're doing next. Build it from the last thing the player said (Rome -> "Che cosa avete mangiato?"; dog -> "Biscotto dorme con te?"). Ban "che cosa pensi del mio lavoro/negozio/X" and "che cosa vuoi sapere (ancora) di me / del mio lavoro" outright, including in keep-chatting.
2. **If the player asked a question or made a remark ("tuo cugino è strano", "la pioggia non mi piace"), react to it first**, even in a few words ("Strano? Mio cugino dice che IO sono strana!").
3. **Use a catchphrase at most once or twice per conversation** (ledger quoting, "orologio indietro"). Vary the opening move: a reaction, an action, a weather remark.
4. **Don't recite the objective phrases or volunteer the info the player is supposed to ask for.** Leave a gap the player fills ("Il mio tempo libero? È un segreto..." only after they ask). In greetings, model the structure on a different object.
5. **Keep keep-chatting on the unit's grammar** (past_did: keep asking "e poi?" / "che cosa hai fatto dopo?"; weather: "e domani?").
6. **Keep lines to 2-3 short sentences.** Keep the weather and current-events remarks, since they were consistently the charming part.
7. Grading: credit weather-yesterday statements of the form "ieri ha fatto caldo / c'era il sole", and don't remove a heart for adverb order, a missing article, or a single hesitant English word inside otherwise correct Italian at A1/A2. Make scene objectives match the character's premise (the shopkeeper with one of everything shouldn't be "holding up two").
