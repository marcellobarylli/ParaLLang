## Overall: does it feel like a conversation you could keep having? (owner complaint 3)

**Mostly yes, more than I expected, with one big tic.** In all six conversations the character asked about me in almost every line (my evening, home, cat, books, sister, gym, grandmother, students, cooking), usually picked up the content of my last line, and kept a thread going into keep-chatting. I wanted to keep going in plans, health, restaurant and work. What breaks it is formulaic structure: every line goes [echo of what I said] + "che bello!" + their own fact + two or three questions + "what do you want to know about my work?". By the third keep-chatting turn it reads like a template, not a person.

## (c) Suggestions for the character at A2

1. **One question per turn**, two only if they are the same question ("what do you cook, and for whom?"). Never put all three objectives in the greeting's question.
2. **Drop the "what do you want to know about my work" tag entirely.** If the player should ask questions, have the character *do* something intriguing (the moving basket, the goldfish with a booking), so the player wants to ask. That worked naturally twice.
3. **No "what do you think of my X / this place" questions.** Swap them for questions about the player's life tied to the topic: "E tu, al ristorante, prendi sempre l'antipasto?" instead of "cosa pensi di questo posto?".
4. **State a character fact once, then move on or develop it** (the pen: "I looked in the box: not there!"), instead of re-quoting the model sentence each turn.
5. **Create the situation the objective names**: Bettina actually asks "Tutto bene?", Bettina actually invites ("Vieni alla festa di Bruno sabato?") instead of "ti invito: che cosa rispondi?". Keep the invitation consistent.
6. **Vary reactions** beyond "che bello!": surprise ("Davvero?"), sympathy ("Mi dispiace!"), mild disagreement, a short anecdote. Answer the player's direct questions first, before asking anything.
7. **Keep weather and setting stable** within one conversation, or comment on the change ("Oh, adesso piove!"). The owner likes weather lines; they land better when the character notices them.
8. Keep what works: the absurd jobs, the world logic (distances in sandwiches, odd numbers are lucky, forms that move at night) and explaining unknown words simply.
