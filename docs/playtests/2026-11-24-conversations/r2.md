## (a) Top 5 problems

1. **The target phrase is handed over before the learner says it: 9 lines outright, 5/5 conversations.**
   - "Tutti dicono: quanto costa! Tu vedi il cappello?"
   - "Un segno dice: gira a sinistra."
   - "Ogni mattina chiedo «che giorno è» e nessuno risponde."

   Usually this happens in the greeting or reply 1, so the learner can simply parrot the line.
2. **Goodbyes are ignored: 4/4.**
   - "Grazie Septimus, arrivederci!" got "Quanti alberi vedi tu?"
   - "Il conto, per favore. Arrivederci!" got "Lei vuole il mio ombrello?"
   - "Ciao Fergus, a dopo!" got "Prendi anche un cornetto?"
3. **Formula repetition and a narrow topic loop.** The echo plus "bene/bravo" opener appears in 19/25 replies, and the same closing question shape repeats within one conversation:
   - The cafe character asks "Lei vuole…?" 4 times.
   - The likes character circles bread and coffee for 6 lines.
   - The numbers character asks "Tu vedi…? / Quanti … vedi?" 4 times.
4. **Harsh or wrong corrections cost hearts: 9/15 graded replies lost a heart, at least 6 of them for accents or punctuation only.**
   - "Si" corrected to "Sì" (5 times).
   - "non ho orologio" corrected to "non ho l'orologio" (worse Italian).
   - "Destra e bene" corrected to "A destra è bene, penso" (unnatural).

   The characters' own lines then use the uncorrected forms ("dove è").
5. **Scene and weather continuity breaks: 2 weather contradictions and 2 setting drifts.**
   - "cielo grigio, like an unpaid bill" becomes "the cielo is clear enough to count".
   - "cross the piazza… reach the tavolo" becomes "Un fulmine lights the finestra… pioggia hits the tetto".
   - The time scene goes from clock tower to shed to awning.

   Small Italian errors appear too: Dorcas, "una donna", says "mi sono perso".

Also seen:
- **Role details from the objectives are dropped.** The likes objective's "two things that look exactly the same" is never set up. The cafe objective asks you to review a meal that was never served. The matchmaker and haggler roles barely reach the dialogue.
- **The narration sprinkles in more and more Italian function words** (e, poi, con, come sempre, già, ancora).

## (b) Best lines and why they worked

- **"Va bene, basta. Il conto non c'è, io non scrivo."** (cafe K2). Pure character: the waiter who never writes orders down has no bill. The words are A1, the joke lands, and it reacts to what I said.
- **"Sì, preferisco il pane, ma non come mio cugino! Lui sbaglia sempre. Ti piace il pane?"** (likes K1). It picks up my teasing, builds a running gag (the always-wrong cousin) and asks about me.
- **"Va bene, vado a destra! Io ho molti biglietti per le città. Dove vuoi andare tu?"** (directions 2). It acts on my instruction, moves the scene on (the ticket window) and asks a personal, answerable question that leads naturally into the next objective.
- **"Bravo, due domande! Oggi è lunedì, credo. Il bar apre alle nove. A che ora arrivi tu?"** (time 3). It answers everything I asked, the "credo" fits a clockwinder who is always behind, and it gives the question back to me.
- **"Bravo! Il cappello costa tre monete. Io non pago mai. Tu hai un cappello?"** (numbers 3). The haggler's royal-warrant joke in four words, then a question about me.
- **"Mi piace il tè la sera. Che cosa ti piace?"** (likes G). The ideal A1 opener: it models the structure with the character's own taste and asks about me, in one short line.

## (c) Suggestions for the character at this level

1. **Elicit, don't dictate.**
   - Set up the situation so the learner needs the phrase, rather than saying it. "Ecco il mio cappello nuovo. È molto bello…" (then stop, so the learner has to ask the price) instead of "Tutti dicono: quanto costa!"
   - Modelling the character's own answer to a personal question ("Io ho cinquanta anni. E tu?") is fine. Saying the exact question the learner must ask is not.
   - If the learner is stuck after a turn, a hint can come then, not in the greeting.
2. **Honour goodbyes in keep-chatting.**
   - When the player says arrivederci, ciao or a dopo, reply with a short in-character farewell and no question. For example: "Arrivederci! E il cappello costa sempre tre monete!"
   - Optionally add a one-line callback joke.
3. **Vary the line shape.**
   - Drop the automatic "X, bene!/Bravo!" echo in most turns.
   - Sometimes react with surprise or disagreement ("Il tè? No! Il tè è triste.").
   - Sometimes say something about the weather or what is happening, and sometimes ask a "why / and you?" question.
   - Don't reuse the same question frame ("Lei vuole…?", "Quanti … vedi?") more than twice per conversation.
   - Widen the topic after two turns: from bread and coffee to music, or the weather, or the cousin.
4. **Put the role's absurd premise in the dialogue, not only in the narration.**
   - The matchmaker should actually propose a match ("Il caffè e il pane: amici? Preferisci il cucchiaio o il cucchiaio?").
   - The haggler should haggle ("Tre monete? No! Due!").
   - That gives the learner something funny to react to with simple words.
5. **Keep scene state consistent and use the objective's set-up.**
   - Fix the weather and the indoor/outdoor setting at the start and keep them.
   - If an objective needs a served dish or two identical objects, have the character produce them before asking for the review or preference.
6. **Grade gently at A1.**
   - Do not take a heart for missing accents, apostrophes or commas only; show the correction without the penalty.
   - Make corrections natural Italian ("non ho un orologio", "va bene") and keep the character's own spelling consistent with them.
