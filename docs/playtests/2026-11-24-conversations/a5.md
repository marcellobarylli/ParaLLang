## (a) Top 5 problems

1. **The "my work" question tail (owner complaint 1 is still present at B1).** Counted in **28 of 36 character lines**
   across all 6 NPCs, either as "cosa pensi del mio lavoro/mestiere" or as "cosa vuoi sapere/chiedere ancora sul mio
   lavoro/ufficio". In keep-chatting turns it becomes a fixed suffix.
   - "Cosa fai quando c'è il sole, e cosa pensi del mio lavoro?" (Griselda)
   - "Che cosa fai con il pesce, e che cosa pensi del mio lavoro?" (Prudence, after I had just finished my story)
   - "Che pizza ti piace, e cosa vuoi sapere ancora sul mio ufficio?" (Horatio, the 3rd turn in a row)
2. **Double and triple questions.** **33 of 36 lines** end with two or more questions, and 7 have three. It often
   pairs a good personal question with the bad "my work" question, so the good one loses force.
   - "Dimmi, che cosa si fa a casa tua, che cosa ti sorprende qui, e che cosa pensi del mio mestiere?" (Gideon)
   - "Che cosa fai tu con la luce, e come finisce la tua serata sulla spiaggia? E che cosa pensi tu del mio lavoro?" (Prudence)
   - "Che cosa fa la signora con il tè freddo, e che cosa dice al gatto? E tu, che cosa vuoi sapere di me e del mio lavoro?" (Prudence)
3. **Giving the target phrases away and quiz-like modelling.** Counted in **10 lines** (all 6 greetings, plus Septimus
   R1 and R2, Prudence R1 and Horatio R1). Characters recite the exact objective phrases, and sometimes complete the
   learner's task for them.
   - "Di solito chiedo «che succede?» e poi dico «ti capisco»." (Septimus, greeting; repeated in R1 and R2)
   - "Un giorno una signora trova una porta... all'improvviso la porta non c'è più, e alla fine..." (Prudence, greeting)
   - "Io racconto così: all'improvviso c'è un rumore, e alla fine è solo un gatto." (Prudence finishes my suspense for me)

   - 3 hard `INVALID_GRADING` errors when I asked "che cosa c'è che non va?" or "che cosa non va?". Only the modelled

   - 3 of 6 conversations resolved early: customs on reply 1, opinions and hypotheses on reply 2. So the "3 graded
     replies" are often only 1 or 2.
5. **Repeated narration and weather continuity.**
   - The "empty chair / pausa for a successor" device appears in essentially every narration line for 3 of the 6 NPCs
     (Griselda, Horatio, Gideon): about 15 lines. It reads like a template rather than a world.

   - One markdown leak (`**panchina**`).

- Septimus: "Grazie, amico mio, ora sono meno triste! ...Che cosa cucini con i libri di cucina, e com'è tuo fratello?"
  He picks up both details I offered and asks about my life.
- Griselda: "Il maggiordomo? Non sono d'accordo, è troppo ovvio!" The character's premise (contrarian) is used on what
  I actually said. It is funny and alive.
- Clementina: "Forse i problemi dormono con la gente." And the narration's "something brassy clears its throat" kept
  my neighbour's trumpet alive in the world.
- Prudence: "Ora ho quaranta inizi e un finale, e il finale è tuo." It pays off her premise using my contribution.
- Septimus: "Quando non c'è un funerale, io sono triste per un amico, perché devo stare in forma." Pure deadpan absurdity
  at the right level.

What these have in common: an echo of the player's words, one in-character absurd twist, and at most one question,
which is about the player or the world.

## (c) Suggestions for this level (B1)

1. **End with one question, about the player or about the world they just described**, never about the NPC's job,
   opinion or work. Drop the "cosa vuoi sapere sul mio lavoro?" tail entirely. If the NPC wants to talk about their
   job, they should *tell* something about it (a funny incident), not ask for a review.
2. **Echo, twist, ask.** React to a concrete detail the player gave (as in the best lines), add one in-character absurd
   comment (contrarian disagreement, a tax on dreams, a mourner keeping in shape), then ask one follow-up about the
   player's detail (the murderer, the concert, the brother).
3. **Model the B1 grammar back instead of reciting the objectives.** Answer "cosa faresti?" with "farei..." and tell
   stories in passato prossimo and imperfetto. Don't put the target phrases in quotation marks ("di solito dico «ti
   capisco»"). Create the situation that calls for them instead: be visibly sad *without* explaining why, so the
   player has a reason to ask.
4. **Accept natural variants in grading:** "che c'è che non va", "cosa non va", "cos'hai?" for "ask what's wrong". Never
   surface `INVALID_GRADING` to the player. Consider recasting subjunctive slips without taking a heart when the
   objective is met.
5. **Pace the objectives:** don't resolve all three on reply 1. Gate them across turns, so the NPC's reply sets up the
   next one (for example, Gideon pushes back on the comparison before asking what surprised you).
6. **Vary the narration** across NPCs: retire the shared "successor / empty chair" motif, or use it once per
   conversation. Keep the weather consistent unless the change is itself a remark, which the owner likes ("the rain
   has gone, which Septimus takes personally" was good as a one-off).
