## (a) Top 5 problems

1. **Goodbyes are refused or ignored: 4 of 5 times.**
   - "Ciao Sidonie, arrivederci!" got "Ma il modulo non è finito, Marco. Aspetti un momento?"
   - "Bene! Molto bene. Arrivederci!" got "...Sei felice?"
   - "Grazie per il caffè. Arrivederci!" got "Vuoi un altro caffè?"

   In a hello/goodbye unit this teaches that goodbye doesn't work.
2. **The same "how are you" move again and again: 5 repeats** (Sidonie #2 and #4; Marigold G, #1 and #4).
   - "Come va oggi?" came right after I had answered "Bene, grazie".
   - "Come stai adesso?"
   - "Stai bene stamattina?"
3. **The target phrase is given away before the learner uses it: 6 lines.**
   - "Anche io sto bene" kills the "ask her how she is" objective.
   - "Arrivederci, a presto! Va bene?" lets the learner just echo it.
   - "Scusi... Non capisco il silenzio. Per favore..." uses all three courtesy targets in the greeting.
4. **The narration is the off-level, repetitive part: in all 4 conversations.**
   - It mixes in Italian words well past A1: sospetto, successore, rimpianto, lentamente, già, registro.
   - The same motif comes back every turn: "pausa for the successor" (7 or more times across Sidonie and Cuthbert), the unused "timbro" (10 or more across Marigold and Leopold), writing the name on the "modulo" (4 times).
   - It contradicts itself: night vs dawn, sunny woods vs a grey sky through a window.
5. **The objectives and corrections work against the scene: 4 instances.**
   - The Fast Talker never talks fast, so "non capisco" has nothing to react to.
   - My early "Scusi... non capisco" did not count.
   - Hearts were lost on "Si"→"Sì" and on "un po' italiano".
   - Hearts were lost on the dubious correction "Sono dell'Inghilterra".

   Smaller things: the same Census Taker role, with the same "missing exactly one" line, came up twice in a row. Sidonie's "Siamo tutti e due bene" is bad Italian.

## (b) Best lines

- **"Bravo, un po' è molto! Qui tutti parlano italiano. E a Londra, parli italiano?"** (Cuthbert): praise with a joke, built on my words, with an easy follow-up.
- **"Io sono di una strada migliore, lontano da qui. Qui piove forte. A Londra piove?"** (Cuthbert): it answers my question, uses the weather, and asks a yes/no question in words I know.
- **"Sì, Marco, parlo molto veloce! Ma per te parlo piano."** (Leopold): it is character, kindness and level awareness in one line.
- **"Buonasera! Salve! Sono Sidonie, e conto le persone. Tu non saluti?"**: a clear, in-character prompt for the objective.

These lines work because they are short, react to what I said, end in one easy question, and use one comic idea that fits the character.

## (c) Suggestions for A1 "first words"

- **Always accept a goodbye.** Answer with a short in-character farewell ("Arrivederci, Marco! Uno in più!") and let the narration close the scene. Never hold the learner back.
- **Do not ask "how are you" more than once.** After it has been answered, move to one other target-word move: "Piacere!", a name game, "Buongiorno o buonasera?", or a weather remark.
- **Leave the learner's objective open rather than doing it for them.** If the learner must ask how she is, hint with body language ("she looks like she has a lot to say about her day") instead of saying "anche io sto bene". Let the learner say goodbye first.
- **Act out the premise that sets up an objective.** The Fast Talker should actually say one fast, run-on line (marked as such) so that "non capisco" makes sense. Accept "scusi" and "non capisco" whenever they fit.
- **At A1, keep the narration in English, or limit it to the unit's words.** Rotate the motifs instead of repeating one every turn, and keep time and weather consistent with the first line.
- **Be gentler with corrections.** Do not take a heart for a missing accent or a small slip at A1; show the correction for free. Prefer natural models ("Sono inglese") over odd ones ("Sono dell'Inghilterra").
- **Keep the dialogue joke in Italian with A1 words** ("un po' è molto", "per te parlo piano"). That is where the charm should be, rather than only in the English narration.
